---
name: Publisher-normed scoring safeguards
description: Evidence and provenance rules for BASC-3, BYI-2, and other publisher-scored assessment instruments.
---

Do not calculate or present publisher-standardized scores unless the applicable licensed edition, language, item mapping, scoring key/norm source, and respondent/form match are verified. Generic RAOS averages, severity bands, inferred reverse coding, and mapping numeric storage codes to option labels by array position are not substitutes.

When authorized scoring materials or source results are unavailable, keep a respondent-separated descriptive record of exact saved labels/codes, show provenance and completeness separately, and block standardized interpretation. Preserve historical responses, existing score rows, and issued reports; do not silently rewrite them.

Do not disable existing RAOS workflow features (including assignment, submission, or “View in English”) unless the user explicitly asks. Add safe parallel views or scoring guards without removing unrelated functionality, and keep translations separate from the original saved responses.

For SDQ, public scoring instructions do not establish permission for electronic administration or scoring. Before calculating totals, impact scores, or norm bands, verify authorization plus the exact edition, language, age/respondent form, item key, and norm table. If these are unverified, preserve exact responses descriptively; do not infer scores, and keep impact, duration, and burden fields separate.

The user confirmed organizational scoring sources/permissions for SDQ, BRIEF-2, BASC-3, and BYI-2 on 2026-10-26, including electronic SDQ scoring. This is not a substitute for the applicable item keys, norm tables, translation provenance, or identity-bound official result data; those still must support each implemented computation.

**Why:** BASC-3, BYI-2, and BRIEF-2 scores depend on authorized, form-specific scoring sources; SDQ’s public scoring guidance is not itself electronic-scoring authorization. Unsupported conversion can produce misleading psychometric conclusions.

**How to apply:** Before enabling standardized scoring, verify applicable authorization, edition, language, respondent match, scoring key/norm source, and human review. If any are unverified, use descriptive responses or verified source-result import; preserve historical records and normal workflow features.

Treat unknown or legacy IDs within these four families (including BREIF misspellings) as instrument-specific, never as generic fallback candidates. Apply the same exclusion to aggregate/product/index views as to score writers.

**Why:** Correcting only the dedicated score endpoint leaves alternate aggregate calculations able to reproduce misleading legacy percentages.
