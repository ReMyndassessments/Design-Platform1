---
name: Chinese literacy observation scope
description: Independent parent and teacher developmental literacy observations, source separation and reporting constraints.
---

The Chinese Literacy Observation Profile has separate teacher-classroom and parent-home instruments for Grades 3–12. One teacher is sufficient for the teacher form; one parent or caregiver is sufficient for the parent form. A second respondent is optional and separate. Neither instrument requires the other, a student questionnaire, interview or individually administered test for submission or manual professional reporting.

**Why:** The user supplied a teacher-only specification, then added an independent parent-home specification on 2026-10-09. Both describe contextual difficulty frequency and educational impact; neither is a dyslexia screener or diagnostic assessment. The parent and teacher versions have not been established as equivalent measures.

**How to apply:** Keep descriptive domains and respondent sources separate; never add an overall index, norms, risk categories, diagnostic conclusions, automated support tiers, cross-teacher averages, parent–teacher pooled scores or discrepancy diagnoses. Label home observations as parent-reported and any school feedback as separately sourced. Preserve incomplete drafts without inventing missing evidence or NA reasons. Limited parental Chinese confidence means Not observed, not Not applicable. External Case Portal synchronization is a separately verified dependency, not a simulated successful sync.

Both parent and teacher forms must offer full English and Simplified Chinese presentation, not a Chinese selector that silently shows English. Keep translation review status honest; Korean remains an English fallback until translated.

**Why:** On 2026-10-09 the user explicitly required English/Chinese availability and objected to the prior English-only fallback. This replaces the earlier decision to withhold Chinese display pending human review.

**How to apply:** Cover instructions, questions, answer anchors, context, evidence, summary and validation. Switching language must preserve answers, IDs, scoring, names and respondent-authored text. NO means insufficient observations, not a negative rating; NA means not applicable and requires a reason.

Both Chinese literacy forms must match the established ReMynd assessment branding, including the branded header and a readable, accessible language selector.

**Why:** The user explicitly objected to these forms having a bare heading and inferior language control compared with the other assessment forms on 2026-10-09.

**How to apply:** Match the established Assessment Portal header shown in the user's reference: dark navy ReMynd branding, respondent identity at the right, and compact En / 中 / 한 language buttons—not a large language dropdown or a separate Student Services brand. Apply this to both respondent versions, previews and completed states.

Both forms must be considered independently by intake analysis and assessment recommendations when Chinese literacy concerns are relevant. Being a Chinese-speaking family alone is not grounds to recommend them.

**Why:** The user explicitly requested on 2026-10-09 that both instruments participate in AI intake analysis and form recommendations, not only manual library assignment.

**How to apply:** Distinguish parent-home and Chinese-language teacher-classroom purposes and IDs in recommendation guidance and visible results. Respect Grades 3–12, verify unknown grades, and do not force a paired battery or automatically send forms. Existing saved analyses require intentional regeneration to change recommendations.

Chinese literacy forms are respondent self-completion questionnaires, not staff-guided or examiner-administered assessments. Parent invitations should use the parent's existing shared assessment portal.

**Why:** On 2026-10-09 the user objected to "Launch Guided" and explicitly requested link sharing, QR codes, or resending the parent portal containing the form.

**How to apply:** Provide parent portal QR/copy/open controls and normal invitation sending. Keep teacher invitations available externally. Do not merge unrelated parents or infer recipient ownership from the parent role alone.

Concrete examples are optional in both parent and teacher forms, including for ratings of 4 or 5. Do not prevent submission because an example is missing.

**Why:** The user explicitly requested removing this submission barrier for the parent and teacher on 2026-10-12, without making respondents redo completed work.

**How to apply:** Keep client and server validation consistent and label optional examples clearly in English and Chinese. Preserve examples already entered.

The staff Chinese literacy review needs an English-view translation toggle for Chinese respondent-authored text; translations are read-only and never replace saved responses.

**Why:** The user requested an English view of the Chinese literacy review on 2026-10-13.

**How to apply:** Show the English/original toggle on every review tab; translate respondent text, report, plan, and monitoring notes for review and English export copies, preserve saved source data, and show failures clearly.

Staff may generate an AI-assisted editable report draft from one submitted Chinese literacy respondent form at a time. Keep parent and teacher evidence separate; never diagnose or release the draft without professional approval.

**Why:** The user asked for the Report tab to generate a report from the submitted form on 2026-10-13.

**How to apply:** Generate only from the selected respondent's submitted record, save it as an unapproved draft, preserve the descriptive/non-diagnostic limitations, and require staff review and approval before release.

AI-assisted Chinese Literacy support plans are drafts from one selected respondent's submitted record and its approved report. They do not combine parent and teacher sources or make automatic clinical/pathway decisions; staff must review before approval.

**Why:** The user requested AI-generated support plans in addition to reports on 2026-10-13.

**How to apply:** Use DeepSeek; keep parent-home and teacher-classroom evidence separate, validate suggestions against the respondent's rated item IDs and permitted strategies, and require staff to select a review date before approving the plan. Keep the generator entry point visible across review tabs and link directly to the report approval gate when needed.

The support plan is a separate step after report approval. The Report tab exports report-only; the final combined report is printed or downloaded from the Support Plan tab after both report and plan approval.

**Why:** The user explicitly clarified on 2026-10-13 that the report must come first, with the separate support plan appended only to the final export from its own page.

**How to apply:** Keep the report narrative free of embedded plan controls or plan content. Preserve domain recommendations and next steps at the end of the report; create and approve the plan separately, then export the report, plan and available follow-up records together.

Each Chinese literacy report must show a rating-distribution chart, interpretation, and support-planning recommendation per selected domain, plus a report-level next-steps section, download, and print.

**Why:** The user requested per-domain charts and explanations plus downloadable and printable reports on 2026-10-13.

**How to apply:** Derive domain results from the selected respondent only; show NO, NA, and unanswered separately; state source/setting scope once, not in every domain. Keep parent and teacher sources separate and non-diagnostic. Point recommendations to the Support plan and keep print/export professionally formatted.

All Chinese Literacy report exports must be in English and printable as PDFs using the same branded report hierarchy. Translate Chinese evidence and any Chinese report, plan, monitoring, or follow-up text with configured DeepSeek in a temporary export copy; never replace saved source wording.

**Why:** The user explicitly required English reports and rejected an export that looked like a broken Word document rather than the report page.

**How to apply:** Skip translation when no Chinese text is present; fail visibly rather than silently exporting Chinese if DeepSeek translation fails. Open a print-formatted page with a clear Print / Save as PDF action and preserve the source records.

Report approval must remain available in any translated display language; approval applies to the saved source-language report, not transient translated text.

**Why:** The user explicitly requested staff be able to approve reports regardless of the language currently displayed on 2026-10-13.

**How to apply:** Do not disable approval in read-only translation views. Keep editing and saving tied to the canonical saved narrative; clearly tell staff which saved report is being approved.

Monitoring and follow-up recommendations must be added to an existing report without rewriting its saved narrative or domain results. Actual delivery logs and completed repeat-form results are separate optional records.

**Why:** On 2026-10-13 a full report regeneration replaced a working approved report when the user had only asked for two recommendations. The prior approved report must remain recoverable.

**How to apply:** Use DeepSeek for only two narrative paragraphs; append them to the exact existing report and preserve all domain results. Archive the previous approved version and require review/approval of the addition. Keep actual-entry and repeat-form tools optional; do not fabricate delivered support, dates, or outcomes.

Do not show historical report versions in the Chinese Literacy review UI. Keep stored versions intact unless the user explicitly asks to delete them.

**Why:** The user asked to remove the Historical versions display and did not request deletion of archived report data.

**How to apply:** Omit the version-history panel from the staff report page while preserving the backend archive and any restore safeguards.
